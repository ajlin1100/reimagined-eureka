Secure Computing & Cybersecurity

Best practices to protect your data, credentials, devices, and communications

1 Identity & Credential Safety

📲︎

MFA Protection & Avoiding Push Fatigue

Always use Multi-Factor Authentication (Duo Push) across university systems. Never approve an unexpected Duo prompt if you did not just attempt to log in. Attackers may spam your phone with push requests ("push fatigue") hoping you hit approve by mistake.

🔑︎

Password Requirements & Recommendations

Directory ID passwords require 8–32 characters (15 max for CorporateTime/Oracle), including uppercase, lowercase, and a number/symbol. They expire every 180 days (90 for OIT staff), with a 10-minute lockout after 6 failed attempts.

Best Practices: Create long, easy-to-remember passphrases (e.g., RHSmith-is-a-gr3@t-school!) using spaces, typos, and symbols. A 7-character password takes 3 hours to crack, while an 8+ character mixed passphrase can take over 2 years! Store non-UMD credentials in a password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password, or Keeper) and never write passwords on sticky notes.

🎣︎

Phishing, Email Security & Legitimacy Checks

Be vigilant against phishing, text scams ("smishing"), and phone impersonations ("vishing"). Attempts will be made to get personal details from you. To confirm an email is genuinely from UMD/DIT, look for these indicators:

  • Personalized Greetings: Official emails address you by name, never generic terms like "user" or "customer."
  • No Direct Links: Legitimate administrative notices won't link directly; they'll direct you to type standard URLs yourself (e.g., account.umd.edu). Never click links in unsolicited emails.
  • PGP Signatures: Official DIT communications contain a PGP digital signature verifiable via the DIT Knowledge Repository.

2 Device, Data & Policy Compliance

🔄︎

Automatic Updates & Antivirus

Enable automatic updates on Windows, macOS, and web browsers (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari) to patch security flaws. Ensure built-in real-time protection is active on personal devices, or download enterprise software via Terpware.

Device Maintenance & Active Directory Policy: All University-owned devices must be powered on and logged into at least once a month to ensure critical security patches and updates are applied. Furthermore, the University's Active Directory (AD) network requires devices to check in at least once every 90 days; devices that fail to check in within this window will be flagged as "dormant" and removed from AD trust relationships.

☁︎

Data Sensitivity & Approved Cloud Storage

Protect university, student (FERPA), and research data by storing it strictly in institutional storage such as UMD Google Drive or Box. Never store confidential UMD data on unapproved consumer platforms or personal unencrypted drives.

🔒︎

Physical Security & Screen Locking

Always lock your computer screen when stepping away from your desk, office, or classroom podium:
• Windows: Press Win + L
• Mac: Press Cmd + Ctrl + Q

⚠︎ Email Exporting & Forwarding Policy

The export or forwarding of work emails and data to external email accounts is strictly prohibited.

Commercial email providers (e.g., @gmail.com, @yahoo.com, @comcast.net) and unsanctioned cloud platforms do not satisfy legal or security requirements for storing PII, FERPA-protected student records, or confidential data. In accordance with the University's IT-14 Standard Institutional Email, forwarding work emails to non-University personal accounts is strictly prohibited. Faculty and staff who transfer data outside authorized channels assume personal legal liability and responsibility for data safety, regulatory compliance, and legal discovery requests. Smith IT will not configure automated transfers to personal systems.

Working Off-Campus? When using untrusted public Wi-Fi networks (airports, hotels, coffee shops), always connect to the GlobalProtect Campus VPN to keep your network connection fully encrypted.
